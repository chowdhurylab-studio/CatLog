Scientific data curation check. Paper {P}. Two readings (A and B) of this paper's enzyme kinetic values are in {D}/{P}/task.json: each reading's full list for the paper (id, metric, value in catalog units kcat s^-1 / Km mM / kcat/Km mM^-1 s^-1, the cell as that reading wrote it, and its enzyme, substrate and variant labels), with "judge": true on the values to judge. The readings are not labelled by source; do not guess who made them.

For EVERY value with "judge": true, decide from the paper and give one verdict:
- measured_here_at_this_scale: this paper measured this value and prints it for this metric, and the value's scale is what the cell and its header print.
- measured_here_other_scale: the paper measured and prints this cell, but the reading has the wrong scale (a lost or inverted exponent, a header multiplier applied the wrong way, a wrong unit prefix). Give the right value.
- as_printed_but_misprint: the value is what the cell and header print, but the paper's own numbers (a Vmax or specific activity with the enzyme's mass, the same measurement printed elsewhere in the paper in another unit, a ratio column that closes only one way) show that the printed scale is a misprint. Say which numbers show it.
- corrected_misprint: the value follows the scale the paper's own numbers support, not the scale the cell and header print. Say which numbers support it.
- repeat_of_one_printed_value: the paper prints this value once and this reading lists it more than once (again under another substrate of the same reaction, or again from a reference row of another table). Give this verdict to the extra listings only: one listing of each printed value keeps its own verdict (prefer the one with "judge": false if there is one).
- not_this_metric: the printed quantity is not this metric: a Vmax or specific activity per mg, a K0.5/S0.5/K' from a Hill fit with n clearly not 1, a Ki, Kd or IC50, a relative or percent value, a rate constant of one step, a constant for one product whose total the paper also gives, or a bound or not-detected entry ("< 0.01", "n.d.") rather than a measured value.
- credited_to_earlier_work: the paper prints the value but attributes it to other work (a citation in the row, the caption or a footnote; "taken from"; a comparison table of other enzymes).
- not_found_in_paper: the paper prints no such number for this metric (computed by the reading, read off a figure, or absent).
- cannot_decide: the text and page images do not settle it; say what is missing.

How to read the paper: {D}/{P}/ holds paper.txt (its first line may be a GLYPH WARNING: the text layer can show the micro sign as m, a superscript minus as 21 or )1, plus-minus as 6), source.pdf and/or source.xml, and paper.supp.txt if present. Where there is a source.pdf, render the pages with the tables (pdftoppm -r 200 -f N -l N -png source.pdf <your folder>/p) into a fresh folder of your own (mktemp -d) and read numbers, exponents and unit prefixes from the page images; where there is none, read source.xml and paper.txt. For a header such as "kcat x 10^-3 (s^-1)" or "kcat (10^3 s^-1)" decide the direction from the paper (text, other tables, kcat/Km against kcat and Km).
Rules: read only {D}/{P}/ and your own temporary folder. Do not open anything else under {D}, nor anything under ~/Documents or ~/Downloads. Judge each value on the paper alone, not on whether the readings agree.

Write {D}/{P}/check1.json as JSON: {"pmid": "{P}", "verdicts": [{"id": "<id>", "verdict": "<one of the nine>", "right_value": "<only for measured_here_other_scale or a misprint: the value and unit>", "evidence": "<page or table, row and column, and the words or numbers that decide; at most 200 characters>"}]} with one entry for every id whose "judge" is true.
