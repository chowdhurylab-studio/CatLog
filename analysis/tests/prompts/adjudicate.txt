Scientific data curation: check claimed enzyme kinetic values against one research paper.

Paper {PMID}. Its files are in {DIR}: paper.txt (the full text, with each table as a "=== Table" block),
paper.supp.txt if present, and the original article (source.pdf or source.xml). If paper.txt starts with a
GLYPH WARNING, its text layer misprints symbols: take every number, unit prefix and exponent from the page image.
You may render PDF pages and view them: pdftoppm -r 200 -f N -l N -png {DIR}/source.pdf /tmp/{RUN}-{PMID}-p

{CLAIMS} lists claimed values for this paper, each in these units: kcat s^-1, Km mM, kcat/Km mM^-1 s^-1. For each
claim, find what the paper prints and give one verdict:

- supported: the paper itself measured this value and prints it, for this metric and this enzyme, organism, variant
  and substrate (and cosubstrate, pH and temperature where the claim gives them). The number agrees after unit
  conversion within 1.5% or within the printed rounding. Synonyms, other spellings, and an isozyme or allele named as
  either the enzyme or the variant are fine. A blank field is fine.
- wrong_number: the paper prints a different number for this value, or the number belongs to another cell or row.
- wrong_scale: the digits are right but a unit, prefix, exponent or header multiplier was applied wrongly (for example
  1000-fold or 60-fold).
- wrong_metric: the printed quantity is not this metric. kcat is the turnover number, including a Vmax the paper
  itself expresses per active site or calls kcat. Km is the Michaelis constant, including an apparent Km. kcat/Km is
  the printed specificity constant. These are not: Ki, Kd, Kact, IC50, a K0.5 or S0.5 from a Hill fit with n clearly
  not 1, a Vmax or specific activity per mg of protein, a ratio to wild type, a constant for one product of a reaction
  whose total the paper also gives, a constant in M^-2 s^-1.
- wrong_identity: the value belongs to another enzyme, organism, variant, substrate or cosubstrate.
- wrong_conditions: the claim's pH or temperature is not the one the paper gives for this value.
- not_measured_here: the paper attributes the value to other work (a citation, "taken from", "as reported").
- not_printed: the paper prints no such number for this value anywhere (it was computed, or read off a figure).
- cannot_decide: the text and the page images do not settle it. Say what is missing.

Write {OUT} as JSON:
{"pmid": "{PMID}", "model": "<your exact model ID, as your system prompt states it>",
 "verdicts": [{"id": "<claim id>", "verdict": "<one of the nine>", "found_at": "<Table 2 row 3, p.5 ...>",
               "on_page_image": false, "reason": "<one line>"}]}
Give one verdict for every claim id. Use only the files in {DIR} and {CLAIMS}. Write nothing except {OUT} and
scratch files named /tmp/{RUN}-{PMID}-*.
