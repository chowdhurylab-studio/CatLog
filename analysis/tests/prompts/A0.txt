Scientific data curation: extract enzyme kinetic constants from one research paper.

Paper {PMID}. Its files are in {DIR}: paper.txt (the full text, with each table as a "=== Table" block),
paper.supp.txt if present (supplementary material), and the original article (source.pdf or source.xml). Where the
text is unclear, look at the original. You may render PDF pages to images and view them:
pdftoppm -r 200 -f N -l N -png {DIR}/source.pdf /tmp/{RUN}-{PMID}-p

Extract every kcat, Km and kcat/Km value that the paper itself measured and prints as a number, for every enzyme,
variant, substrate and condition, from the tables and the text. One row per value: a table row that gives kcat, Km
and kcat/Km is three rows. Do not compute values the paper does not print. Skip values printed only as a bound
(< or >) or as not detected.

Convert each value yourself: kcat in s^-1, Km in mM, kcat/Km in mM^-1 s^-1.

Write {OUT} as JSON:
{"pmid": "{PMID}", "model": "<your exact model ID, as your system prompt states it>", "note": "", "rows": [
  {"metric": "kcat | Km | kcat/Km",
   "value": <the number in the unit below>, "unit": "s^-1 | mM | mM^-1 s^-1",
   "raw_value": "<the value as printed>", "raw_unit": "<the unit as printed>",
   "enzyme": "", "organism": "", "variant": "<wild type, or the mutation(s)>",
   "substrate": "", "cosubstrate": "",
   "pH": <number or null>, "temperature_C": <number or null>,
   "conditions": "", "location": "<Table 2, Figure 3 legend, Results ...>"}]}
If the paper reports no such value, write "rows": [] and say why in "note".

Check the file with: python3 {VALIDATE} {OUT}
Correct anything it reports. Use only the files in {DIR}. Write nothing except {OUT} and scratch files named
/tmp/{RUN}-{PMID}-*.
